# 0005 — Lock-free Buffer: frozen iteration behind a runway

A Sync reads Samples **straight out of the ring Buffer, with no lock and no intermediate copy**. The Buffer tracks one monotonic count of Samples ever written, published with a release store; a reader freezes that count once with a single acquire load at iterator init and then walks plain array indices against that fixed bound. The ring reserves a **runway** of 4 physical slots — the live window stops that many slots short of the write head — so the producer cannot overwrite a slot the reader has yet to reach. Overrun is **prevented by that margin and never checked at runtime**; the Buffer mutex, the `buffer_snapshot()` query and the 34.6 KB Sync scratch are deleted.

## Why

**The mutex bought very little and the copy cost a lot.** Today the Sync thread takes a mutex, ages *every* stored Sample into a scratch array, unlocks, then streams. At the v1 parameters (`CONFIG_KUUKI_SAMPLE_INTERVAL_SEC=900`, `CONFIG_KUUKI_RETENTION_SEC=2592000`) that is 2880 Samples × 12 bytes = 34,560 bytes of ring, plus a second 34,560-byte scratch of aged copies: the Retention target is paid for twice in RAM, on a part where RAM is the binding constraint. The lock is also contagious — it occupies a parameter in both `ble_start()` and `sampler_start()`, drags a `struct k_mutex` forward declaration into two otherwise hardware-free headers, and has to be owned and passed by the application wiring — and a second, unrelated scheduler lock exists in the Sync control write purely to publish the Latched read instant and the High-water mark as an untearable pair (one store neither thread can observe half-updated).

**The concurrency is strictly single-producer / single-consumer (SPSC) on a single core.** The Pod is a XIAO nRF52840 — one Cortex-M4F. Exactly one producer (the sampler thread, at a Sample tick) and exactly one consumer (the BLE Sync thread) touch the ring, and they never run simultaneously; the consumer is only ever *preempted* by the producer. There is no second observer, so the adversary is not a cache but the **compiler**: nothing else would stop it sinking a slot write past the counter store that publishes it. That is why the counter is accessed with `__atomic` acquire/release at its two use sites rather than with barriers or `volatile`. `volatile` is the wrong tool — it stops the load being hoisted out of a loop but says nothing about the ordering *between* the slot write and the counter store, which is the whole guarantee. Acquire/release says exactly that, costs at most one barrier per Sample tick and one per Sync, and stays inside the implementation file so `buffer.h` remains plain C that both host suites compile directly.

**Freezing the end makes the reader's job trivial.** After that one acquire load every step is plain indexing against an immutable bound: no per-record synchronisation to get wrong, and no way for the batch to grow under the reader mid-stream. That last property is also what keeps the Age subtraction safe — the Latched read instant is read from a monotonic clock *after* the freeze, so every Sample in the batch has a capture time at or before the latch and the unsigned subtraction cannot underflow. The freeze and the latch therefore have to sit at one visible call site, in that order.

## The stall budget

The producer advances the write head **one slot per Sample tick**; the consumer walks the whole ring in seconds. The runway is therefore not a deadline on the Sync but a budget for how long the consumer may be *stalled* — and it is spent only while stalled, because the gap between the two positions is monotone: a blocked consumer holds its position while the producer advances at most one slot per tick, so the gap only ever shrinks, never jumps. Total tolerance is the whole runway, not a per-record allowance:

```
stall budget = runway × Sample interval = 4 × 900 s = 3600 s
```

The only unbounded-looking wait in the Sync **stream** is the notification back-off in `sync_notify()`: a `bt_gatt_notify()` returning `-ENOMEM` (the small ACL buffer pool transiently exhausted by a burst) sleeps 10 ms and retries. Every *other* error, including the connection dropping, ends the stream. So the loop cannot outlive the connection, and a link that has stopped making progress is torn down by the BLE **supervision timeout**, whose Bluetooth Core-spec ceiling is 32 s (`connSupervisionTimeout` maxes at `0x0C80` × 10 ms). The Pod does not raise Zephyr's peripheral preference and the central picks the actual value, so 32 s is the worst case rather than the expected one.

```
3600 s / 32 s ≈ 112×
```

That is the margin, and it is the number to re-check — not to take on faith — if either side of it moves. The runway is 4 rather than 1 because it costs 48 bytes, 0.14% of the ring: buying two orders of magnitude of headroom that cheaply is not a trade worth optimising. The backing array *grows* by the runway so the Retention target is unchanged; shortening a product promise to pay for a synchronisation detail would be the wrong way round.

**The floor this must clear is ≥ 10× the ceiling** — `runway × interval ≥ 320 s`, which at a 4-slot runway means a Sample tick of ≥ 80 s. That is the concrete form of the build-time guard below; the v1 cadence clears it by 11×. Anyone re-tuning the runway or the tick has the whole inequality here.

## Considered options

- **Per-record overrun detection with a 1-slot runway** — rejected, and this was the reviewing recommendation, so the disagreement is on the record. Re-reading the counter per record and aborting the stream if the producer has caught up would make overrun *detected* rather than impossible. It was declined because the branch is unreachable by the 112× above: it adds an atomic load and a compare to every record on the hot path, and — worse — an error path no test can legitimately reach, so it would be dead code that *looks* live. The accepted design compensates with the larger runway plus the build-time assertion. A reader who weighs an unreachable-branch cost differently should be able to reverse this from the numbers above without re-deriving the analysis: take the check, drop the runway to 1, and the RAM difference is 36 bytes.
- **Keep the mutex, drop only the scratch** — rejected: streaming under the lock would hold it across the whole notification stream (seconds, and unbounded if the back-off spins), stalling a Sample tick. The lock exists to make a *fast copy* safe; it cannot be stretched over a slow stream.
- **Zephyr's `k_msgq` / `k_fifo` or a stock SPSC ring** — rejected: they are queues, and a queue read *consumes*. The Buffer must instead serve its whole Retention target repeatedly — the newest ~30 days survive an arbitrary number of Syncs, and overflow overwrites the oldest.
- **Double-buffering, or a seqlock (a version counter the reader re-checks, retrying if the writer intervened)** — rejected: both keep the second copy, which is the RAM cost the whole change exists to remove.
- **A threaded stress test for the ordering** — rejected under `native_sim`: it would be non-deterministic and would pass whether or not the acquire/release pairing is right, which is false confidence in precisely the property it appears to test. The pairing is verified by reading the two use sites; the frozen-batch and usable-depth properties are behavioural and are tested single-threaded by interleaving producer calls with iterator calls.

## Consequences

- **The residual hazard is configuration drift, not a race.** `CONFIG_KUUKI_SAMPLE_INTERVAL_SEC` has Kconfig `range 1 86400`, and at the low end the budget collapses: a 5-second tick leaves a 20-second stall budget against the 32-second ceiling, and the producer can lap the reader. That is not hypothetical — a short tick paired with a shortened `CONFIG_KUUKI_RETENTION_SEC` is the shape of a bench or demo build, and it is the one such configuration that still fits in RAM. Unlike overrun itself this mistake is *reachable*, so it gets a `BUILD_ASSERT` in `app_config.h`, beside the one already tying the Retention target to the tick, enforcing the ≥ 10× floor derived above.
- **Four slots and one release store have no visible runtime partner.** That is the point of this record. There is no overrun check to find, and there never was one; the runway *is* the mechanism. Deleting either as unused re-introduces a data race that no test will catch.
- **A ring must be longer than the runway.** `capacity > runway` becomes a Buffer precondition, and usable depth is `storage length − runway`.
- **Age leaves the Buffer, which invalidates a glossary entry.** The iterator yields raw Samples; the aged, wire-shaped record type moves into the Sync module next to the record size and the encoder, because Age is a property of a Sample *as seen by one Sync*, not of a buffered Sample. This **contradicts `CONTEXT.md`'s Buffer headword**, which says the Buffer "only ever hands over *everything* it holds, aged against the Latched read instant" — now wrong twice over: it hands over everything held *at the freeze*, and it ages nothing. `buffer.h`'s module comment claims the same. Both are corrected as part of the implementing change rather than left to disagree with this record.
- **Nothing changes on the wire.** Same records, same oldest-first order (ADR-0002), same end-of-batch marker, same sentinel — `docs/wire-contract.md` and the webapp are untouched. Net RAM change is about −34,512 bytes.
- **"Runway", not "guard band".** Sync already calls its half-Sample-interval Mark tolerance a guard band (`sync.h`), an unrelated mechanism; the Buffer's reserved slots are called a runway everywhere to keep the two apart. Neither is domain vocabulary: the runway is an implementation detail no user, webapp or wire concept touches, so it deliberately gets **no `CONTEXT.md` headword**.
